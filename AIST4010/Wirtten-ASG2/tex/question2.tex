Both 2D and 3D convolutions exploit locality and parameter sharing, yielding translation equivariance under appropriate padding/stride. The decisive difference is the domain and sweep: 2D operates on H$\times$W (images) and slides over two axes, whereas 3D operates on H$\times$W$\times$D/T (volumes or videos) and slides over three, thus capturing volumetric or spatio-temporal patterns. This extra dimension raises computation and memory cost and can heighten overfitting risk with limited data; common mitigations include factorized (2+1)D or separable spatio-temporal designs. Consequently, 2D is preferred for spatial cues (edges, textures), while 3D is advantageous when motion continuity or 3D structure is intrinsic to the task.
